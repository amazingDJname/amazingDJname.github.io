How to read the results

Ways a chord can be "in tune", and the marks

most fused: all notes are low harmonics of one fundamental, so the chord sounds as one spectrum; measured by the largest harmonic number. simplest dyads: every pair of notes forms a simple, tunable interval, whether or not they share a fundamental (the "complexity" figure, lower is simpler). easiest route: the hardest interval anyone has to tune along the tuning route is the easiest among the readings. least retuning: smallest total movement from 12-TET. fewest signs: fewest HEJI comma signs to engrave. The overtone family is ranked by fusion; every other family by complexity. A mark is relative to the chord's own readings.

Shortlist view

The recommended reading is the purest one (lowest dyad complexity); the alternatives are the most fused acceptable reading and the best of another family. Each card's difficulty score, used to order ties and to rank when nothing qualifies: 1.5 × the most comma signs on any one note + 0.5 × signs per note + 1.3 × the hardest interval anyone must tune (Tenney height of the interval as sounded, 7/4 = 4.95, 16/9 = 7.17, 19/16 = 12.1; a note within 6¢ of 12-TET counts as 3, since it is simply played as on the piano) + 2 per respelled note + the largest move from the piano in cents ÷ 20 + 2 if a prime above 7 is used (+3 above 13) + 3 × the reading's complexity above the chord's best, as a fraction. The chips are a notation-and-tuning load estimate for the players to judge, never a filter: easy = at most one sign per note, hardest interval ≤ 6.3, nothing moves more than 35¢, your spelling kept, 7-limit; fine = two signs somewhere, or an interval up to 9, or up to 45¢, or 11/13; hard beyond that.

Live MIDI, performer window, Max

MIDI in listens to a keyboard (Chrome on your own machine, or inside Max's jweb); the chord you hold is read and tuned at once by the live-pick rule, and the sustain pedal (CC 64) captures it to the list. The performer window shows only the selected chord, large, and follows the main window through this browser's storage; inside Max it can be driven directly with a show message. When the page runs inside jweb, every selection is sent out as selection <json> and hz …, and the page accepts chords, midi, capture, select, focus and show messages; see max/README-max.md in the repository.

Complexity

For each interval, the exponents of its primes above 2 times log2 of the prime, with primes above 7 weighted more (×1.6 for 11 and 13, up to ×2.3 for 31). Summed over all pairs, counting pairs with the root twice and with the bass one and a half times. Tuning routes and the "hardest to tune" figures use the same height counting the 2s as well, so an interval's actual width matters.

Root

Suggested by Parncutt's root-support weights: a chord tone a unison, fifth, major third, minor seventh or major second above the candidate supports it by 10, 5, 3, 2, 1; the bass gets 1.5 extra. Type any pitch class to try an absent root.

Cents and Hz

Deviations from 12-TET with the chosen note held at its tempered pitch. To anchor elsewhere, add one constant to every value.

Progression (two or more chords)

Chords are linked in the order typed. A common tone (same written note, or the same pitch class an octave away) is held at the same frequency; when several are shared, the one whose hold breaks the others least is chosen, and the breaks are reported with the comma they amount to. With no common tone the root moves by the simplest just ratio within tolerance of the tempered motion, spelled as you spelled the roots (C→F♯ gives 10/7, C→G♭ gives 7/5). Each root's position against 12-TET is tracked as an exact ratio, so the net drift of a progression that returns to its first root comes out as a named comma (the I–vi–ii–V–I pump in 5-limit returns 80/81, a syntonic comma flat). A chord left at 12-TET is fixed; a reset link re-anchors the next root at its tempered pitch. Suggest searches the readings of every chord (beam width 40) for the path minimising Σ|breaks| + 8·(each reading's complexity above its chord's best, as a fraction); the drift-avoiding variant adds 0.5¢ of cost per cent of root drift at every chord.

Playback

An additive tone (24 harmonics, 1/n amplitudes with a gentle high-frequency roll-off, no vibrato). The level is scaled by the number of voices (n−0.75) with a mild tilt that lifts high notes and tames low ones, and a limiter at −3 dBFS guards the output, so a full chord does not clip. Single notes are therefore a little louder than the chord, per voice.

HEJI

Letter = Pythagorean pitch; each sign is one comma, up or down, for one prime. Derived from the comma definitions, so confirm glyphs against the Plainsound legend before engraving.
